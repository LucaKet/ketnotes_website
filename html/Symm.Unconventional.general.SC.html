Symmetry of unconventional superconductivity (general)

To describe a Definition: unconventional superconductivity based on symmetry considerations one proceeds as follows: (taken mostly from Superfluid He3 and unconventional superconductivity (Annett, Ch. 7))

  1. Define the BdG hamiltonian using a $k$ and spin dependent coupling term: \[ \Delta_{ab} = (I \Delta(k) + D(k) \times \ \sigma )i \sigma_y \tag{1} \] so that you can distinguish between an even part, pertaining to spin singlet (Two-particle state: spin) \[ \Delta(k) = \Delta(-k) \tag{2} \] and an odd part pertaining to spin triplet states: \[ D(k) = -D(-k) \tag{3} \]

  2. Select, based on experimental results, which type of pairing mechanism you need (possibly both)

  3. Expand the coefficients (the three components of the) $D(k)$ vector and/or $\Delta(k)$ as \[ \Delta(k) = \sum_{(\Gamma, m)} \gamma_{(\Gamma, m)} f_{(\Gamma, m)}(\vec{k}) \tag{4} \] where $\Gamma$ is the irrep of the symmetry group of dimension $d$, $m \in [1, \dots, d]$. The same expansion is to be carried out for the $3$ components of the $D(k)$ vector.

  4. Somehow find out which irrep is the right one to consider. Now the problem has been reduced to finding the elements of the $\gamma$ coefficient matrix.
