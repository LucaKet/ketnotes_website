Definition: Weyl semimetals

A Weyl Semi-metal is a system that displays a similar structure to the one of a Dirac points in 3D, with one important difference being that the hamiltonian, in $3+1$D looks like \[ H = \vec{k} \ \cdot \ \vec{\sigma}, \tag{1} \] meaning that we have a $2 \times 2$ matrix which already “uses up” all three Pauli Matrices. This implies that $H$ can't be gapped out by simply adding a term proportional to say $\sigma_z$ (which is what one can do in a $2$D Dirac system like graphene, see Gapping Dirac points with a mass term).

The excitations so created are

  • Gapless,
  • Of definite non-zero chirality,

and are called Weyl fermions (this is because the hamiltonian is referred to as Weyl hamiltonian)

$2$ Weyl Fermions can not be destroyed by adding a small perturbation to $H$, rather the perturbation has to be large enough for the two Weyl points to merge. The resulting excitation would then be a non-chiral Dirac cone.

One can reverse this argument to say that to get a Weyl semi-metal (a system in which the excitation at gapless points are Weyl fermions) one needs to break time and/or inversion symmetry in a Dirac semi-metal, so that the single Dirac excitation may split into $2$ Weyl ones.
