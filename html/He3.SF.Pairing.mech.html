He3 superfluidity: pairing mechanism

Once a SC phase was found in He3, a suitable Pairing mechanism has to be identified. There are no excitations that might play the role phonons do in standard BCS theory, and the normal particle-particle Van der Waals forces is too weak at high distances to account for SC.

The idea came from Landau's theory [Note:], as it was recognized that the interactions in He3 lead to a $3$-fold increase in the magnetic susceptibility (with respect to the non interacting system from which Landau's theory is built upon). This suggested that the material might behave almost like a ferromagnet, thus neighbouring particles could have attractive interactions due to the energetically more favoured spin-aligned configuration.

Indeed, making the assumption of small momentum transfer between the equivalent of Cooper pairs, one finds that the potential can be written as \[ V(k,k') = g^{-1} \ \frac{4 Z_0}{4 + Z_0} \ \times \ \hat{s}_k \cdot \hat{s}_{k'} \tag{1} \]

where the important part is the product between the $2$ spin operators $\hat{s}_k \ \hat{s}_{k'}$, representing (in the Spin as Pauli matrices representation of Spin) the spin of the $2$ particles constituting the new formed bound state.

This is the physical insight needed to specify which quantities go into/are important when following the standard approach (Symmetry of unconventional superconductivity (general)) in constructing the theory for an unconv. SC. Note: there is an important difference in the construction of the theory of He3 SF, namely that we replace the irrep $\Gamma$ construction with the simpler expansion of $D(k)$ in terms of spherical harmonics, thus the role played by $\Gamma$ is here instead played by the quantum number $l$, (and $m \ \in \ [-l, \dots, l]$)
