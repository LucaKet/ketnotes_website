Kramers degeneracy theorem

Statement: In a system of spin $1/2$ particles (or in general half integer spin) that has time reversal (TR) symmetry, each state is doubly degenerate; meaning that for each eigenenergy $E(k)$ there exists $2$ eigenstates $|\psi\rangle$ and $|\phi\rangle$ that both have $E(k)$ as eigenvalue.

Proof: Having TR symm. means that \[ [H, T] = [T, H] = 0 \tag{1} \] where $T$ is a Time-reversal representation in appropriate operator form. Consequently if $|\psi\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $H$, so will also be $|T\psi\rangle$. Now compute \[ \langle\psi|T\psi\rangle: \tag{2} \] using the anti-unitary nature of $T$ we may write: \[ \langle\psi|T \psi\rangle = \langle T T \psi |T\psi \rangle = - \langle\psi|T\psi\rangle \tag{3} \] where in the first equality we used $\langle v|u\rangle = \langle Tu|Tv\rangle$, in the second $T T = -1$. We now have two orthogonal states (inner product is $0$) which have the same energy $\rightarrow$ we have proved Kramer's theorem.
