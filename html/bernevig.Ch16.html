Topological superconductors in one and two dimensions (Bernevig, Ch. 16)

A. Bernevig, “Topological insulators and topological superconductors”, Ch. 16: topological superconductors in one and two dimensions.

pg194: The first step in the construction of the BdG Hamiltonian is to double the dof! this means going from a description in terms of creation and destruction operators (each with their spin and momentum) to one where the main object is the spinor \[ \psi = (C_{p, up}, C_{p, down}, C^\dagger_{-p, up}, C^\dagger_{-p, down}) \tag{1} \] which contains creation operators of $-p$ momentum and destruction operators of momentum $p$ with all $4$ possible combinations of spin.

pg194: written this way the hamiltonian has a symmetry with respect to the operator \[ C = \tau_x \otimes 1_{2\times2} \tag{2} \] namely \[ C H(-p) C^{-1} = -H(p) \ \ \leftrightarrow \ \ H(p) \rightarrow -H(-p) \ \text{under } C \tag{3} \] this can be easily seen by realizing that

  • $C H(-p)$ has the block structure of $C$, with the lower block of $H$ in the (0,1) block of $C$ and the higher block of $H$ in the (1,0) block of $C$
  • $C^{-1} = C$, thus the second matrix multiplication gives back $H$, but with the blocks reversed

this symmetry is known as particle–hole symmetry, as it exchanges the sign of energy and momentum (see Particle-hole symmetry of the BdG Hamiltonian).

pg196: The pairing potential for the case of spinless (spin polarized) fermions can not be of s-wave type! The pairing in the spinless fermion case is between positive and negative momentum particles: \[ H_\delta = \Delta(p) C^\dagger_p C^\dagger_{-p} + \mathrm{h.c.} \ \ ; \ \ (C^\dagger_p)^* = C_{-p} \tag{4} \]

pg196: One must take care and notice what the $\psi$ operator looks like that makes the hamiltonian be a $2\times2$ matrix: in the case of SC it has creation and annihilation operators for particles of the same kind, in the Dirac insulators they are annihilation operators of particles of different kind. This is important because otherwise the hamiltonian looks similar (or the same in the $m \rightarrow \infty$ limit), thus it gives the physical interpretation of the difference between these systems. The form of $\psi$ is the result of the redundancy in the construction of the BdG hamiltonian.
