BdG Hamiltonian

The idea behind the construction of the BdG hamiltonian is that, once you double the system using $2$ degenerate energy levels in your description (twice $\epsilon(p)$ and twice $-\epsilon(-p)$) you reveal the true nature of the SC quasi-particles. You are able to write the system compactly this way thanks to the Creation and annihilation operator commutation rules This procedure artificially introduces a symmetry: Particle-hole symmetry of the BdG Hamiltonian

This is because, by doubling the system in this way, you are able to uncover that once a pairing is introduced between the system's standard excitations (of the form that the Cooper pairing requires) the new hamiltonian is such that its creation and annihilation operators (and thus its excitations) are a mixture of the original ones.

The mixture is $\sim$ equal parts each individual excitation when $\epsilon \sim \ 0$, whereas it becomes once again un-paired at high energies where the coupling is no longer effective

Further one recovers that the description in terms of this doubled system is redundant as, of the $4$ operators one needs to define in this formalism, only $2$ create independent particles, the other $2$ being just their hermitian conjugates.

The explicit construction of the hamiltonian is shown — for example — in Sato's review on topological SC, section 3 or in Topological superconductors in one and two dimensions (Bernevig, Ch. 16).
