Topologically stable gapless phases of time-reversal-invariant superconductors (Beri, 2010)

B. Beri, “Topologically stable gapless phases of time-reversal-invariant superconductors”, Phys. Rev. B 81 (2010), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.81.134515.

Combination of electron hole (EH) + time reversal is called chiral symmetry, which is a unitary operator $C = C^\dagger$:

→ $C \,\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BdG}}\, C^\dagger = -\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{BdG}}$ (notice the minus sign).

in the basis where $C \equiv C' = \mathrm{diag}(1,-1)$, the BdG hamiltonian looks like

\[ H'_{\mathrm{BdG}} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & D(k) \\ D^\dagger(k) & 0 \end{pmatrix} \tag{1} \]

and $\det D(k) \neq 0$ for all $k$ ↔ a gapped system.

In the case of a gapped system, homotopy arguments based on topological invariants can still be used to understand when the Fermi surface is stable against perturbations.

Wen & Zee had already studied properties of Fermi surfaces of TI systems in $d=2$ (2002 PRB paper [TBD: add link once a note is created]).

Main result of the paper:

“Take a $d$-dimensional system. The Fermi surface (FS) of dimension $d-p$ (meaning a $d-p$ cut of the $d-1$ dimensional FS, think e.g. of a $d=3$, the FS is $2$D, but you can look at a $1$D FS by cutting the FS at a fixed $k_z$) will be such that $\det D(k) \neq 0$ in the $p$ dimension transverse to it. Now take a path of dimension $p-1$ (i.e. a sphere of dim $p-1$: $S^{p-1}$) and encircle the point $k_0$ at which $\det D(k) = 0$ (i.e. the FS). With this we have a map from $S^{p-1}$ to $GL(N,\mathbb{C})$ [Q: what is this?!?]. If this map is non-trivial in the homotopy group $\pi_{p-1}[GL(N,\mathbb{C})]$ no deformation of $D(k)$ (→ of $H$) can lead to the vanishing of the FS: it is stable. Such non-trivial maps (‘homotopy group’ (?)) exist only for $p=2$:

\[ \pi_1 [GL(N,\mathbb{C})] = \mathbb{Z}. \tag{2} \]

[Note: understand what these map/homotopy groups are]

→ $d$-dimensional SC can support $d-2$ stable FS.”

note1: $GL(N,\mathbb{C}) \equiv$ the space of invertible $N\times N$ matrices. Here $N$ is the dim of the normal-state hamiltonian (pre BdG).

The topological invariant characterizing this charge can be thus expressed as a winding number of the phase of $\det D(k)$ as a FS is encircled by a path $C$, i.e. (citing Wen & Zee):

\[ q = \frac{i}{4\pi} \oint \mathrm{Tr}\left[ C' \,(H'_{\mathrm{BdG}})^{-1} \nabla_l H'_{\mathrm{BdG}} \right] dl \tag{3} \]

where $H'_{\mathrm{BdG}}$ is the matrix put in off diagonal form as before (eq.1).

Sec III: consequences in 2D systems

Depending on the spin-rotation symmetries of the system (see: Altland–Zirnbauer classification of TI topological superconductors) all possible different cases are discussed: DIII, AIII, CI.

The detailed argument looks at all cases and considers fermi points (FP) ($d = 2$, $p = 2 \Rightarrow \dim(\mathrm{FS}) = 0$) that either lie or don’t lie on time reversal invariant momenta (TRIM). The end result is that, in all cases the total number of FP has to be a multiple of $4$.

Note: one way around this is to have FP on TRIM in classes CI or DIII, in which case you get twice degenerate FP with charge $|q|=2$, which only need to come in pairs. But then any small perturbation will split these FP into $2$ that don’t lie on TRIM and that have charge $|q| = 1$, getting back to multiples of $4$ total FP.

Surface states of Topo SC

One wishes to relate the bulk topological invariant $\nu$ which makes the material topological in the first place to some properties on its surface. $\nu$ has to be even in class CI, and can take any integer value in the other $2$. However on the surface of the SC, when placed in contact with another material that has topological charge $\mu$ (which can be $0$, e.g. for the vacuum), one can define the net topological charge as $q_{\mathrm{net}} = \nu - \mu$.

This is indeed related to the number of surface FP: while you might be able to create $N > q_{\mathrm{net}}$ FP by spawning vortex anti-vortex pairs, you will never have less than $q_{\mathrm{net}}$ surface FP, as this net winding counts the number of unpaired FP (which are allowed to begin with only because we are considering a surface theory, as is the case for Fermion doubling theorem violation in topological insulators).
