A primer on Weyl Semimetals (Kar & Jayannavar, 2019)

S. Kar and A. M. Jayannavar, “A primer on Weyl Semimetals: on the discovery of topological phases”.

pg5: Chern numbers are defined — generically — as integrals over the BZ of the Berry curvature of all the bands of the system: \[ \nu = \sum_n \int_{BZ} F_n \ dk \tag{1} \] where $F_n$ is the Berry curvature as a magnetic field of the $n$-th band. They are topological invariant, meaning they are robust to adiabatic changes of the hamiltonian.

The Berry curvature in graphene becomes $0$ when Time reversal and Inversion symmetry are preserved, which means that $H(t) = H(-t)$ and $H(r) = H(-r)$. When these symmetries are broken, the total surface integral of the Berry potential (ergo the Berry curvature) is still zero, but Dirac points (points in k-space where the dispersion is linear) contribute with $\pm \pi$ each.

pg7: Weyl Semimetals are $3$D analogues of graphene. They are materials in which conduction and valence band touch linearly at certain points in k-space. There is one big difference with graphene: being $3$D, these hamiltonians look like: \[ H = k_x \sigma_x + k_y \sigma_y + k_z \sigma_z \tag{2} \] thus there is no way to add a term which gaps the spectrum. This is instead possible with graphene, where adding a $\mathrm{const} \ \sigma_z$ term gaps out the spectrum. (note that const takes the interpretation of a mass if the theory is viewed through a field theoretic lense).

pg8: The mass does indeed have an effect in Weyl Semimetals: sending it to zero we get that the hamiltonian, in its most general form, commutes with the chirality operator. This means that the Weyl fermions have a chirality, either $+1$ or $-1$.

pg9: When two Weyl points of opposite chirality merge (through perturbation of the hamiltonian) they give rise to a Dirac point, which has $0$ chirality.

The Berry curvature is singular where Weyl points touch → the WP can be considered as monopoles for the Berry curvature (which is defined to begin with in analogy with the magnetic field Berry curvature as a magnetic field).

pg11: Fermi arcs are — in general — closed contours in k-space of constant energy (equal to the Fermi energy). In the case of WSM they are also joining the projections of the WP on the Fermi energy.
