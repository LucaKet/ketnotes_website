Fermion doubling theorem

In a given (standard) BZ $2$ fermionic degeneracies must be created if the topological invariants are to remain constant.

An example of this is when a Dirac points ($0$ chirality) splits into $2$ distinct Weyl points, whose hamiltonian commutes with the Chirality operator and which thus have either $+1$ or $-1$ chirality, the total value having to remain constant.

This can be seen as the result of the identification of the left-right and upper-lower bounds of the BZ. By then integrating (Berry potential) over a closed line following the borders of the BZ one gets opposite contributions from each edge. Thus the total integral must be zero.
