Kramers degeneracy

Consider a spin $1/2$ system that has Time reversal invariance (T). This means \[ T H(k) T = H(-k) \tag{1} \] $T$ is an anti-unitary operator: \[ T^2 = -1 \tag{2} \] thus, for two eigenstates u,v: \[ \langle Tu|Tv\rangle = -\langle Tv|Tu\rangle = -\langle Tv|-TT|Tu\rangle = \langle v|u\rangle \tag{3} \] meaning that $\langle Tu|u\rangle = \langle u|Tu\rangle$, but $\langle Tu|u\rangle = \langle u|Tu\rangle \rightarrow \langle Tu|u\rangle = 0$.

Thus in a spin $1/2$ system with time reversal invariance there is always a double degeneracy: the two eigenstates u, Tu (which are distinct since their overlap is zero) have the same energy (because the system is T invariant → they diagonalize H at the same time).
