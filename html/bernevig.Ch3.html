Hall conductance and Chern numbers (Bernevig, Ch. 3)

A. Bernevig, “Topological insulators and topological superconductors”, Ch. 3: Hall conductance and Chern numbers.

pg15: The Berry phase is an integral over a $1$D line, thus in CMP it is relevant whenever a $1$D surface appears. This can be a single band (of a $1$D system (wire)) or a $1$D Fermi surface of a $2$D system. The Berry field is a $2$D vector and it is integrated over $2$D surfaces. It is thus relevant for filled $2$ bands BZ and for $2$D Fermi surfaces of $3$D metals. Key idea: the dimensionality of the system imposes the structure we use to gain information on it.

pg23: When computing the electrical conductivity one neglects the diamagnetic contribution of the current operator, which is \[ ne \ \delta_{ij} \delta(x-x')\delta(t-t') \tag{1} \] $n$ being the charge carrying density, not because the contribution is small but because it is diagonal in space indices, thus does not contribute to topological invariants.

For other physical properties of metals/SC it can be very relevant.
