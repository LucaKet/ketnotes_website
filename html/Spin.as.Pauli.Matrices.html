Spin as Pauli matrices

One can express the spin of a particle using the Pauli matrix representation: \[ \hat{s} \cdot \vec{\sigma} \tag{1} \] where $\hat{s}$ is a vector in $3$ dimensional space pointing in the direction in which the particle spins. $\vec{\sigma} = (\sigma_x, \sigma_y, \sigma_z)$ is the usual vector of $2\times2$ Pauli matrices.

N.B.: we know this is the correct way to represent spin in quantum mechanics due to the underlying group theoretic explanation: spin must be represented by operators satisfying specific commutation relation rules. In two dimension (in spin space, meaning a particle that can only spin “up” or “down” in some arbitrary direction in real space) these operators are Pauli matrices.
