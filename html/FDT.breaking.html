Breaking the fermion doubling theorem

There are various ways in which the Fermion doubling theorem might be broken. One is to consider different types of “BZ” in non-hermitian systems, where the symmetries at the borders are different to the ones we are used to, e.g. the upper band might be seen as equivalent to minus the lower one, thus giving in the line integral the same (rather than opposite) contribution. This is what happens in a Klein-Bottle-like geometry (see paper by Lukas König).
