Berry curvature as a magnetic field

In analogy with E&M, one can take the Gauge dependent definition of the Berry potential Berry potential and define a Gauge independent quantity, referred to as Berry curvature.

It is defined — once again as the $B$ field is defined as the curl of the E&M potential — starting from the curl of the Berry potential: \[ V = \nabla \times (-i A) \tag{1} \] where the “-i” is a convention, $A$ is the Berry potential. from this vector $V$ one obtains the Berry curvature by contraction with the Levi-Civita symbol: \[ V_i = \epsilon_{ijk} \ F_{jk} \ \ \rightarrow \ \ F_{jk} \equiv \langle\nabla_j n | \nabla_k n\rangle - (j \leftrightarrow k) \tag{2} \]

Note: The math makes more sense once you remember that \[ V_i = \nabla_j \langle n|\nabla_k|n\rangle - (j \leftrightarrow k) = 2 \left( \langle\nabla_j n | \nabla_k n\rangle - (j \leftrightarrow k) \right) \tag{3} \]
