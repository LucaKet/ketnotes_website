Dirac points in 3D

One possible way to generalize the $2$D Hamiltonian of a Dirac point to three spatial dimensions is as follows \[ H = v_f \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{k} \\ \vec{\sigma}\cdot\vec{k} & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \tag{1} \] which, as was the case in $2$D, is susceptible to being gapped out by the addition of a constant term of the form \[ \Delta (1 \otimes \ \sigma_{z}), \tag{2} \] where now we need the extra $2\times2$ identity matrix (1) because we are living in a $4$ dimensional space.

Once again symmetries usually prevent such a term from appearing, thus keeping the system gapped.

These systems are what is called a Dirac semi-metal, which are in some sense the precursors to Definition: Weyl semimetals
