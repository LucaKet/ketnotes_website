Particle-hole symmetry of the BdG Hamiltonian

The artificial doubling of the system in the construction of the BdG Hamiltonian introduces a new “symmetry”. (air quotes as the symmetry is only a consequence of the doubling of the system). Namely one finds that the Hamiltonian so defined respects \[ -H(k) = C H(-k) C^{-1} \tag{1} \] where \[ C = (\tau_x \otimes 1_{2\times2}) K, \tag{2} \] $K$ being the complex conjugation operator. This symmetry becomes nonetheless manifestly physical once the excitations of the SC are studied, as they are indeed particle-antiparticle mixtures as described in BdG Hamiltonian.

Looking at the BdG equation: \[ H(k) [u(k), v^*(-k)]^T = E(k) \cdot [u(k), v^*(-k)]^T, \tag{3} \] which may also be written after the application of the $C$ operator: we start by writing the equation for $-k$ instead of $k$ and by inserting identities in the form of $CC^{-1}$ (calling $[u(k), v^*(-k)]^T = \vec{v}(k)$): \[ C^{-1} [C (H(-k))C^{-1}] C \vec{v}(-k) = E(-k) \vec{v}(-k) \tag{4} \] \[ C^{-1} (-H(k)) C\vec{v}(-k) = E(-k) \vec{v}(-k) \tag{5} \] and finally, by passing $-C^{-1}$ to the r.h.s. as $-C$ acting on $\vec{v}(-k)$, we obtain: \[ H(k) C\vec{v}(-k) = -E(-k) C\vec{v}(-k). \tag{6} \] This implies that the four (or eventually $4N$, where $N$ is the number of orbital d.o.f.) eigen-energies are not independent, rather they are \[ E_1(k), E_2(k), -E_1(-k), -E_2(-k); \tag{7} \] in light of the particle hole symmetry with which the hamiltonian was constructed to begin with you only need to find $2$ eigen energies to have access to the full spectrum.

Furthermore, this implies an important feature of the spectrum of the hamiltonian, namely that it will necessarily be symmetric with respect to $E = 0$! This is an important result, which forces us to find a quantity (topological invariant) to differentiate hamiltonians that undergo gap closing different from just counting states below the $0$ energy line (this number remains constant, because if one band crosses from above, there will be another crossing from below). The resulting quantity is called Pfaffian (Pfaffian invariant)
