Berry phase (Bernevig, Ch. 2)

A. Bernevig, “Topological insulators and topological superconductors”, Ch. 2: Berry phase.

General idea: as an hamiltonian undergoes a cyclic and adiabatic variation of its parameters its eigenstates gain a physical phase, called Berry phase. One can write down a formula from direct calculation for such a phase, obtaining: \[ \gamma_n = i \int_{C} dR \ \langle n(R)|\nabla_R|n(R)\rangle \tag{1} \] ($n$ is the band index, $C$ the closed loop in parameter space).

This same formula can be re-written in $2$ ways, by introducing $2$ auxiliary quantities:

Berry potential

This is simply taking the initial definition and giving a new name to the quantity in the integral: \[ A(R) \equiv i \ \langle n(R)|\nabla_R|n(R)\rangle \tag{2} \] so that now the Berry phase is: \[ \gamma_n = \int_{C} dR \ A_n(R) \tag{3} \]

Berry vector and curvature

The definition of the Berry potential is similar to that of the E&M potential $F$, from which the magnetic field is defined as \[ B = \nabla \times F. \tag{4} \] We then define the Berry vector as: \[ V = \nabla \times (-i A) \ \rightarrow \ V_i = \nabla_j A_k - \nabla_k A_j \tag{5} \] and consequently we define the Berry Curvature as: \[ F_{jk} = \langle \nabla_j n(R) | \nabla_k | n(R)\rangle - \langle \nabla_k n(R) | \nabla_j | n(R)\rangle \tag{6} \] N.B.: Bernevig confounds these $2$ calling them both “Berry curvature”. But in truth to go from one to the other you have to contract with the Levi-Civita symbol: \[ V_i = \epsilon_{ijk} F_{jk} \ \rightarrow \ V_1 = F_{23} - F_{32} \tag{7} \] and the math works out remembering that: \[ \nabla_j A_k = \langle \nabla_j n | \nabla_k n\rangle - \langle \nabla_k n | \nabla_j n\rangle \tag{8} \]

pg10: Eigenstates of topologically non trivial systems are points where they are not well defined, with consequences for the Chern number of the system.

pg11: Monopoles (points in parameter space where the eigenstates are not well defined) are sources and drains of the Berry curvature, meaning that the integral of the Berry curvature over a sphere containing these monopoles gives $2\pi$. The integral over a number $\nu$ of monopoles will give $2\pi\nu$, we thus identify $\nu$ as a Chern number.

pg13: Berry phase is used to describe electrons with spin moving in a magnetic field. It is shown that as the $B$ field is made to vary (in direction, while keeping the magnitude constant) a spinful electron gains a Berry phase which can be experimentally detected and — more importantly — can give rise to the sign change of the wave function in the same way exchanging the spinors does.
