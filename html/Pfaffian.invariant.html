Pfaffian invariant

The Pfaffian invariant is a topological invariant used for the characterization of topologically distinct BdG Hamiltonian that (by construction) have particle hole symm.

Let us first discuss the physical origin of this quantity, before going about its actual calculation. In a BdG hamiltonian the number of electrons is not conserved (there are terms proportional to $2$ creation / $2$ annihilation operators with different coefficients), what is conserved is the parity: the number of electrons remains even/odd under the action of $H_{bdg}$. What this is implying is that, if the hamiltonian is adiabatically changed in such a way to make it favourable to create a single fermionic excitation, then the number of fermions would indeed change parity, and the two hamiltonians (before and after the adiabatic change) should be considered topologically distinct. Of course the fermion created in this way won't be a normal electron, it instead has to be a superposition of electron and hole. This is what we call Bogoliubov quasi-particle, which is codified by the creation operator \[ b^\dagger = (u C^\dagger \ + \ v C) \tag{1} \] ($C$ and $C^\dagger$ being the electronic annihilation/creation operators respectively). Such operators diagonalize the hamiltonian, and have eigenergies: \[ b^\dagger \ \rightarrow \ E, \qquad b \ \rightarrow \ -E \tag{2} \] so that the creation of such a quasiparticle is equivalent to the destruction of its respective hole.

All this to say that the correct quantity to calculate is the parity of the number of fermions, which changes when a band crossing appears in the hamiltonian because it becomes favourable to create a Bogoliubov quasiparticle which consequently changes the fermion parity of the system.

To compute the Pfaffian invariant one proceeds by first rendering $H_{bdg}$ anti-symmetric, then the invariant is defined as \[ Q = \mathrm{sign}[\mathrm{Pf}(i H)] \tag{3} \] where $Pf$ is the Pfaffian operator, $H$ is the anti-symmetric BdG hamiltonian. The Pfaffian operator (very roughly) computes the square root of the determinant of the matrix, which by virtue of its PH symmetry will be \[ \pm \ i (E_0 \times \ E_1 \times \ \dots \ \times \ E_N) \tag{4} \] where the sign is uniquely defined such that it changes when the fermionic parity changes. This is exactly what we wanted, and thus what we use when identifying topologically distinct BdG hamiltonians
