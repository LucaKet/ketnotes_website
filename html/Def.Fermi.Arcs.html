Definition: Fermi arcs

Fermi arcs and Weyl nodes are the (connected) defining features of a Weyl semi-metal.

Fermi arcs are zero-energy “Fermi surfaces” which terminate on the projections of Weyl nodes of opposite ends of the system.

They are in general a direct consequence of the fact that momentum is a good quantum number (Weyl semimetals require translational invariance), thus one can have states for momenta at which bulk states are not present even though the entire system is gapless. Namely one can have states between Weyl points.

These can be understood as follows: take a $2$D Weyl semimetal, it will have $4$ Weyl nodes which form a connected fermi surface. Now start pulling apart the top and bottom layers of the system, $2$ cones will stick to one surface and $2$ to the other. The cones previously part of the same surface are now connected to each other, the connection is what we call a Fermi arc.

These Fermi arcs are thus related to surface states of the system.
