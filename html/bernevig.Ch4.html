Time-reversal symmetry (Bernevig, Ch. 4)

A. Bernevig, “Topological insulators and topological superconductors”, Ch. 4: time-reversal symmetry.

pg34: Finding a representation for the time reversal operator $T$. To do so we start by noting that \[ T x T^{-1} = x \ \ ; \ \ T p T^{-1} = -p \tag{1} \] and thus \[ T [x,p] T^{-1} = -[x,p] \tag{2} \] since the commutator $[x,p] = i\hbar$ we get \[ T i T^{-1} = -i, \tag{3} \] the time reversal operator is proportional to charge conjugation $K$! It will in general be written as \[ T = UK \tag{4} \] with $U$ a unitary operator. The form of $U$ is related to the spin dependence of the particles we are studying.

Spinless particles

What we find in this simple situation is that the action on the creation/annihilation operators is \[ T c_j T^{-1} = c_j \tag{5} \] and in the Fourier transform we get \[ T c_k T^{-1} = c_{-k} \tag{6} \] thus when acting on a T-symmetric hamiltonian you find: \[ T H T^{-1} = \sum_{k} c^{\dagger}_{-k} \ T h(k) T^{-1} \ c_{-k} = H \ \ \leftrightarrow \ \ T h(k) T^{-1} = h(-k) \tag{7} \]

N.B. a spinless system with TR symm will have $0$ Hall Conductance because the Berry field is \[ F_{ij}(-k) = -F_{ij}(k) \tag{8} \] and thus the integral over the entire BZ vanishes.

Spinful particles

The representation for the time-reversal operator now takes a spin operator into account: \[ T S T^{-1} = -S \tag{9} \] so we represent this via a rotation of $\pi$ along an arbitrary axis, conventionally the $y$ axis \[ T = \exp(-i \pi S_y) K \tag{10} \] in the case of spin = $1/2$ particles we can choose the Pauli matrices as the spin operators thus \[ S_y = \frac{\hbar}{2\pi} \ \sigma_y \tag{11} \]

Kramer's theorem

Shows that a TR symm. system of half integer spin is necessarily doubly degenerate, meaning that there will always be at least two distinct eigenstates of the hamiltonian that both have the same energy $E$ (see Kramers degeneracy). To show this, first remember that, by definition, if a system has TR symmetry it means that \[ [T,H] = 0 \tag{12} \] and thus that if $|\psi\rangle$ is an eigenstate of $H$ so will be $T|\psi\rangle$. it is then enough to show that \[ \langle T\psi | \psi \rangle = 0 \tag{13} \] to infer that the two states are indeed distinct and thus that there must be a double degeneracy.
