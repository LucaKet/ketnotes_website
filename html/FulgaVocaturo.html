Sec II: definition of Weyl point

A Weyl semimetal is a metal (conduction and valence band touch at some $k$) but the bands only touch at a point in k-space, thus metallic behaviour is limited.

Further these points where the bands are touching must respect the following to be considered Weyl points: If the BZ is sliced in $2$D planes, on each plane the spectrum of the hamiltonian will have a gap. By following the spectrum along the third $k$, the gap will at some point close. At each $k$ the spectrum can be characterized as topological or trivial by calculating the Chern number. The point (in k-space) at which the spectrum closes must also be a point at which the system goes from topological to trivial (or vice-versa) in order for it to be considered a Weyl point.

N.B.: in periodic systems these points appear in pairs, each having opposite chirality.

Sec VI: Superconductivity

Superconductivity on the surface. Superconductivity requires pairing which happens on the Fermi arcs.

Superconductivity pairing must be achieved via the action of matrices. These matrices are in turn chosen by imposing that they maintain the crystal symmetry. In particular, for a given group which represents the crystal symmetry, one looks at the irreducible representations of such group. Each has an effect on the band structure and/or the other symmetry properties of the system. One then chooses accordingly to what the experiments say which irrep to use.
